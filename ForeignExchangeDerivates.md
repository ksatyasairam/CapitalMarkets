# Foreign Exchange Derivates

Here are some common foreign exchange (FX) derivatives that are listed and traded in financial markets:<Br>

**_FX Futures:_** These are standardized contracts to buy or sell a specific amount of a currency at a predetermined price on a set future date. They are traded on exchanges like the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) and are used for hedging or speculating on currency movements.<Br>

**_FX Options:_** These give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to exchange currency at a specified rate before a certain date. Options can be used to hedge against adverse currency movements or to speculate on future currency fluctuations.<Br>

**_FX Swaps:_** These involve the exchange of two currencies on a specific date at a rate agreed upon at the inception of the contract, followed by a reverse exchange at a later date. FX swaps are commonly used for managing short-term liquidity needs.<Br>

**_Currency Swaps:_** These are agreements to exchange principal and interest payments in one currency for principal and interest payments in another currency. They are typically used to manage long-term exposure to currency risk.<Br>

**_Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs):_** These are forward contracts where the physical delivery of currency is not required. Instead, the difference between the contracted forward rate and the prevailing spot rate is settled in cash. NDFs are often used in markets where currency controls restrict the free flow of currencies.<Br>

**_FX Forwards:_** These are customized contracts between two parties to exchange currencies at a specified rate on a future date. Unlike futures, forwards are not standardized and are traded over-the-counter (OTC).<Br>

These derivatives are essential tools for managing currency risk, facilitating international trade, and enabling speculative opportunities in the FX markets.
