# CapitalMarkets
Capital markets derivatives are financial contracts whose value is derived from an underlying asset, such as stocks, bonds, commodities, currencies, interest rates, or market indexes. Here are the main types of derivatives:

## Options: 
These give the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specific price within a certain period. There are two types: American options (exercisable anytime before expiration) and European options (exercisable only at expiration)1.

## Futures: 
Standardized contracts obligating the buyer to purchase, or the seller to sell, an asset at a predetermined future date and price. Futures are traded on exchanges and are highly liquid1.

## Forwards: 
Similar to futures but traded over-the-counter (OTC), making them customizable to the needs of the parties involved. They are not standardized or regulated like futures1.

## Swaps: 
Contracts in which two parties exchange financial obligations, such as interest rate swaps where fixed interest payments are exchanged for floating-rate payments1.

Derivatives are used for various purposes, including hedging (reducing risk), speculation (betting on price movements), and arbitrage (profiting from price differences in different markets)
