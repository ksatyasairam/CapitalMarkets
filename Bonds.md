# Bonds
A bond is a fixed-income instrument that represents a loan made by an investor to a borrower, typically a corporation or government. Here are some key aspects of bonds:

**Issuer:** The entity that borrows the money by issuing the bond. This can be a government, municipality, or corporation.<Br>
**Face Value:** The amount the bond will be worth at maturity, and the amount the issuer agrees to pay back. It's also known as the par value.<Br>
**Coupon Rate:** The interest rate the issuer pays to the bondholder. This is usually a fixed rate paid periodically.<Br>
**Maturity Date:** The date when the bond's principal amount is due to be paid back to the bondholder.<Br>
**Yield:** The rate of return on the bond, which can vary based on the bond's price and interest payments.<Br>

Bonds are used by entities to finance projects and operations. Investors buy bonds to receive regular interest payments and to get their principal back at maturity




# The relationship between bond prices, interest rates, and yields:

## Bond Prices and Interest Rates:
**Inverse Relationship:** When interest rates rise, bond prices typically fall, and when interest rates fall, bond prices usually rise 1. This happens because new bonds are issued with higher interest rates when rates go up, making existing bonds with lower rates less attractive. Conversely, when rates drop, existing bonds with higher rates become more attractive.<Br>

## Bond Prices and Yields:
**Inverse Relationship:** Bond yields and prices also have an inverse relationship. If the price of a bond increases, its yield decreases, and if the price decreases, its yield increases 2 1. This is because the yield is calculated based on the bond's annual interest payment divided by its current price.<Br>

## Interest Rates and Yields:
**Direct Relationship:** When interest rates rise, new bonds are issued with higher yields to attract investors. Therefore, the yield on existing bonds must adjust to remain competitive, which often means the price of existing bonds will drop.<Br>

### To summarize:
Higher Interest Rates → Lower Bond Prices → Higher Yields<Br>
Lower Interest Rates → Higher Bond Prices → Lower Yields<Br>

**Note:** This dynamic ensures that bonds remain competitive in the market relative to current interest rates and investor expectations
